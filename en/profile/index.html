MY PROFILE
Noia, Galicia · 2003
CAETANO
HORTA POMBO
📍 Noia, Galicia · Club do Mar Noia

I'm Caetano Horta Pombo, an Olympic rower and rowing coach. I was born in Noia in 2003 and my first sporting experiences were on a motorbike. I raced motocross at regional and national level while combining that passion with my first rowing sessions.

At 15 I joined the Galician High Performance Sports Centre (CGTD), a key stage in my life when I left motorbikes behind to become a professional rower.

In 2020 came my first big international result: Junior European Champion. Just a year later, aged 18, I earned an Olympic diploma (top-8 finish) in the lightweight double sculls at the Tokyo 2020 Games.

From there, established in the senior team, I set the Spanish lightweight erg record (6:06), earned a second Olympic diploma at Paris 2024 and won several World Cup medals.

My lightweight years ended at the U23 World Championships in Canada, where I won gold in the single scull.

Since 2025 I have rowed in the heavyweight team, with my eyes on the Los Angeles 2028 Olympics. In 2026 I started a new double sculls partnership with Rodrigo Conde and we won silver at the European Championships in Varese, my first senior European medal.

Beyond competing, I keep studying different areas of sport. I love learning, understanding why things work and constantly improving. All those experiences, in sport and in life, have shaped my own method, which I now want to share with you.

MY STORY
From motocross to Olympic rowing
﻿
15 January 2003 — Noia, Galicia
Where it all began
I was born in Noia, a town on the Galician coast with a long rowing tradition. From a young age I was naturally curious about sport and competition.
~2008 — Motocross
Motorbikes, my first love
Before I discovered the water, motocross was my passion. That mindset of controlled risk, chasing the perfect line and handling pressure from such a young age has been key to getting where I am today.
2012 — First strokes
My uncle, the river and me
At 9, my uncle — a coach at Club do Mar Noia — took me to the river. The idea was to get stronger for motocross. But the water hooked me. I went to my first races and started doing well. Too well to ignore.
~2017 — CGTD Pontevedra
The step into high-performance sport
When I was accepted into the Galician High Performance Sports Centre at 14, I had to give up motocross for good to combine school with elite training. It wasn't easy, but I knew rowing was my path. My progress there was rapid.
2019 — Junior Worlds in Tokyo
5th in the world as an under-17
In my second year as an under-17 I finished 5th at the Junior World Championships in Tokyo.
2020 — First European gold
Junior European Champion
At 17 I won my first international medal: gold at the Junior European Championships in the double sculls.
2021 — Tokyo, Japan
My first Olympic Games at 18
While preparing for my university entrance exams, I earned a seat in the lightweight double that would race at the Tokyo Olympics, with Manel Balastegui. We finished seventh, winning an epic B final and taking home an Olympic diploma.
2023 — A historic record
The fastest erg in Spain
At 20 I broke the Spanish lightweight 2000 m record on the Concept2 RowErg, with 6:06.9. I beat the previous record by 0.7 seconds and became the fastest lightweight rower in Spanish history.
2024 — Paris & Canada
Olympics + U23 World Champion
A dream year. In August I earned another Olympic diploma in Paris and, a few weeks later, travelled to Canada for the U23 World Championships, where I won gold in the lightweight single. A historic year to close one chapter and open another.
2025 — Shanghai, China
Senior heavyweight debut: new cycle, new challenge
With the Olympic cycle towards Los Angeles 2028 just underway, I joined the national heavyweight team in the quadruple sculls with Teixidor, Jofre and García. At the World Championships in Shanghai, our comeback in the B final earned us 8th in the world. The road to LA2028 has started strong.
2026 — Varese, Italy
European silver: my first senior European medal
A new double sculls partnership with Rodrigo Conde. After a season of learning together at the World Cups, we reached the European Championships in Varese and won silver in 6:06.22, just two seconds behind Serbia's gold and ahead of the Olympic champion Sinković brothers. My first senior European medal and confirmation that we are on the right course for LA2028.
